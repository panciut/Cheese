[TXT] STAGES 2–3
[TXT] A controlled vocabulary, tailor-made
[TXT] Why custom | ● Italian NLP tools err: panna→panno, latte→latto. Our stoplist keeps the intensifiers that matter: «molto», «poco», «leggermente» | ● Deterministic normalization: abbreviation + typo maps, accent restoration, measurement units dropped | ● Singular↔plural merge only when both forms are attested → antonyms stay split (carico ≠ scarico) | ● Top-200 lemmas + top-80 bigrams per attribute (freq ≥ 3): audit basis + style anchor in the prompt, never a closed dictionary
[NOTES] 'Stages 2 and 3 build one controlled vocabulary for each of the seven\n\xa0 attributes: the most frequent lemmas and the most frequent bigrams,\n\xa0 obtained with a tailor-made Italian lemmatisation.\n\x0b\n\xa0 Why not a standard NLP library? Because on the sensory lexicon they err\n\xa0 systematically — "panna" (cream) becomes "panno" (cloth), "latte" (milk)\n\xa0 becomes "latto" — and, worse, they treat as stopwords the very words that\n\xa0 carry meaning here: "molto", "poco", "leggermente" — very, little, slightly\n\xa0 — are sensory intensifiers, not noise. So the pipeline is fully\n\xa0 deterministic and reproducible: an accent-aware tokeniser, hand-built maps\n\xa0 that expand abbreviations and fix audit-found typos, removal of pure\n\xa0 measurement tokens (mm, cm, %), and accent restoration applied only when the\n\xa0 accented form already exists in the corpus, to avoid false positives.\n\x0b\n\xa0 Lemmatisation deliberately avoids generic suffix-stripping. A curated\n\xa0 dictionary handles the ambiguous cases, and singular-plural / gender\n\xa0 variants are merged only when both forms are actually attested in the\n\xa0 corpus, with the more frequent form winning. This single rule is what keeps\n\xa0 antonyms apart — "carico" is never merged with "scarico", "equilibrato"\n\xa0 never with "squilibrato" — while still collapsing "cotto/cotta/cotti/cotte"\n\xa0 into one lemma. Adverbs in -mente are kept whole, since they are meaningful.\n\x0b\n\xa0 The chart shows the vocabulary breadth per attribute: paste structure is the\n\xa0 richest, with around 780 lemmas over more than 40,000 tokens; rind thickness\n\xa0 is the poorest, around 250 lemmas, consistent with its narrower descriptive\n\xa0 range. Each vocabulary keeps the top-200 lemmas (frequency ≥ 3) and top-80\n\xa0 bigrams. It serves two purposes: a quantitative basis for the audit, and a\n\xa0 stylistic anchor in the LLM prompt — the top-60 lemmas per attribute — used\n\xa0 to suggest the panel\'s real wording, not as a closed dictionary, since the\n\xa0 model already speaks Italian.'

[TXT] STAGE 4 · KEY REQUIREMENT
[TXT] From numbers to quality: the rind
[TXT] Hundreds of captions were just measures («10», «1 cm», «8-10 mm»). A deterministic function converts everything to mm and assigns a bucket.
[NOTES] 'Stage 4 tackles head-on the brief\'s explicit requirement: replacing quantitative descriptions with qualitative ones.\xa0\nThe emblematic case is rind thickness, where hundreds of captions were plain numbers: "10", "1 cm", "on average 9 mm", "8-10 mm".\n\nThe solution is a deterministic function that recognises fully numeric captions, converts everything to millimetres, with a heuristic reading values below 5 as centimetres, and assigns a qualitative bucket per the table you see: below 8 millimetres "very thin", up to 10 "thin", and so on up to "very thick" beyond 18.\x0b\nWhy do it deterministically and not with the LLM? Because it eliminates at the root the model\'s main source of inconsistency, which otherwise bucketed "1 cm" and "10 mm", the exact same physical measure differently.\xa0\x0b\nIn the chart on the right you see the result: 424 numeric rows neatly collapsed into five buckets.\xa0\n\nMixed captions, those blending numbers and description, are instead left to the LLM because they require contextual reasoning.\n\x0b'

[TXT] THE KEY RESULT
[TXT] The shuffle test: does the model use the image?
[TXT] How it works | Predictions are shuffled across test rows, breaking the prediction ↔ image alignment.  | Recompute 100 times → null distribution → z-score. z > 3 ⟺ p < 0.001: strong evidence of image-conditioning.
[TXT] What it says | ● m1 (ResNet): z≈0 everywhere → pure language model | ● m3 / m6 (ViT): use the image on 4/7 attributes | ● Odore, Spessore, Colore, Struttura → succeed | ● Aroma → no model uses the image
[NOTES] "We're at the most important result of the whole project. The question is as simple as it is\nfundamental: is the model actually using the image, or just mimicking the caption distribution?\nThe tool is the shuffle test. The idea: if a model is truly conditioned on the image, its prediction\nfor image i must match the reference of i better than a randomly picked prediction. So we shuffle the\npredictions across test rows, breaking the alignment, recompute the overlap a hundred times to get a\nnull distribution, and measure the z-score. A z above 3 corresponds to p below 0.001: strong\nevidence.\nThe verdict, in the chart, is crystal clear. m1, the ResNet model, has z near zero everywhere: it\nnever uses the image, it's effectively a pure language model guessing from the distribution. m3 and\nm6, the ViT models, clearly use the image on four attributes out of seven: smell, thickness, colour\nand structure. The only one where no model uses the image is aroma.\nKeep this chart in mind: the next slide draws the architectural conclusion."
